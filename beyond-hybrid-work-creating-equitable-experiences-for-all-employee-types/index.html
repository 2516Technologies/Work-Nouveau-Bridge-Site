Beyond Hybrid Work: Creating Equitable Experiences for All Employee Types

Creating Equitable Experiences for Every Employee

Are your remote employees feeling disconnected while in-office staff get “proximity bias” benefits? Workplace equity isn’t just about fairness. It directly impacts retention, innovation, and productivity. And you don’t need a massive HR department to start making changes.

Hybrid work was once seen as a temporary pandemic solution. But today, it’s clear flexible work is here to stay. Gallup reports that 77% of remote-capable employees expect hybrid or fully remote options.

But as with any change, new challenges have emerged. In this case, we’re inequity between employees based on where they work. 

The Hidden Costs of Inequity

There’s a real  impact of workplace divides. Microsoft found that 43% of remote employees feel left out of key meetings and decisions. And the Harvard Business Review shows in-office employees often gain recognition and promotions simply by being physically present. “Out of sight, out of mind,” right?

And these aren’t just random data points. For small and mid-sized organizations, this creates real risks, including talent walking out the door, innovation roadblocks, and productivity waste.  

How Can You Create Equity

Equitable workplaces don’t happen by accident. Here’s how forward-thinking organizations are tackling the challenge:

  1. Remote participants often end up on the sidelines in hybrid meetings. So reconsider how you work together. Implement digital-first protocols where everyone joins from their own device, no matter where they are. Or assign someone in physical meetings to monitor chat, raise remote voices, and ensure all participants are included.
  2. Make all contributions visible. Empower your managers to highlight contributions rather than physical presence, and to regularly celebrate achievements across all work arrangements. Pair employees from different locations to expand networks beyond physical proximity and drive collaboration.
  3. Remote workers often get fewer promotions despite strong performance. Level the playing field by basing promotions on outcomes, not visibility, and recognized capabilities instead of relationship-based influence. (This requires us to be aware of our own biases, which are often tricky to weed out.)
  4. Be intentional about building culture across boundaries, which requires deliberate effort. Ask yourself how you can ensure remote employees have access to social experiences similar to in-office perks, or how to create virtual spaces for spontaneous conversations that anyone can join?

Equitable experiences don’t require a huge budget, just intentional systems. And organizations that intentionally design equity win in recruiting, retention, and innovation. Theirs are the employees who contribute fully, develop professionally, and connect to the mission.
